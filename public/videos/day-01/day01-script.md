# Day 1: Build Stronger Support for Your Knees

**Status:** DRAFT. Not approved for release. Clinical content, movement quality, and safety copy all still need review.
**App:** Mobility, a 60-day mobility and strength program
**Audience:** Adults aged 50–70, including beginners
**Planned runtime:** About 7:58 (478 s)
**Tone:** Warm, calm, reassuring, and plain-spoken. This is not a gym workout and not a medical advertisement.

---

## Source and originality note

- **Primary concept reference:** Tone and Tighten, a knee-strengthening video presented by Dr. Jared, PT. It covers how the knee bends and straightens, how the hip and ankle influence the knee, why strengthening matters as well as stretching, and slow, deliberate technique. Its five exercises (single-leg bridge, lateral hip hike, lateral band walk, single-leg balance with reaches, single-leg deadlift) are kept for the **future exercise library only**. None of them appear in Day 1.
- **Day 1 exercise sources:** the seated knee-strengthening transcript, the Ed Deboo home-strengthening transcript, and the functional-strength and sit-to-stand transcripts the producer supplied earlier.
- **Transcripts on file:** none of the source transcripts were attached to this production session or stored in the repository. The narration below is original writing based on the principles in the brief. Before clinical sign-off, a reviewer should compare it against the original transcripts.
- No narration, footage, likeness, or branding from Tone and Tighten or Dr. Jared is reproduced.
- The script does **not** claim that most knee pain comes from weakness. It says that knee discomfort has many causes and that strengthening is one way to support everyday function.

## Scene 2: Education (0:40–1:45)

**Visuals:** clean motion-graphics diagrams in the Mobility palette (warm off-white, sage green, deep green-grey). Motion graphics are recommended over AI video because they keep the anatomy accurate. Simplified leg line art, with no gore and no realistic muscle rendering.

- S2-B (0:55–1:12): the hip, knee, and ankle light up in sequence along one line. Labels: `Hip`, `Knee`, `Ankle`
- S2-C (1:12–1:30): soft highlighted regions at the hip and the front and back of the thigh. Label: `Muscles around the hip and thigh`
- S2-D (1:30–1:45): three simple icons (chair, counter, sit-to-stand) preview today's session.

**On-screen text**
- 1:12: `Knee discomfort can have many causes. This video does not diagnose them.`
- 1:32: `Today: slow · controlled · comfortable range`

**Narration**
> [0:42] Your knee works mostly like a hinge. It bends and straightens as you sit down, stand up, walk, and climb stairs. But it doesn't work on its own. Above it is your hip, and below it is your ankle. How these joints move, and how well the surrounding muscles support them, can influence how your knee is guided through each step. Knee discomfort can have many different causes, and this video can't tell you what is behind yours. What we can do is gently build strength in the muscles around your hips and thighs. Stretching has its place, but strengthening helps those muscles support you during everyday movement. We'll keep every movement slow, controlled, and within a range that feels comfortable for you.

## Scene 3: Seated Knee Extension (1:45–3:50)

**Lower-third title (on screen for the whole scene):** `Seated Knee Extension · 1 set × 5 per side · DRAFT`

**Visuals**
- S3-A (1:45–2:05): wide three-quarter view of a sturdy wooden chair with no wheels, back against the wall. The instructor walks in and sits tall toward the front of the seat.
- S3-B (2:05–2:30): static seated setup with feet flat, hands on the seat edge or thighs. Overlay arrows: `feet flat`, `sit tall`, `chair against wall`.
- S3-C (2:30–3:00): **right leg**, one-rep loop clip played 5 times (5 × 6 s), on the same locked-off three-quarter angle with no cuts mid-rep. A rep counter `1/5 … 5/5` appears at the start of each rep.
- S3-D (3:00–3:12): the right foot rests on the floor and the instructor resets.
- S3-E (3:12–3:42): **left leg**, one-rep loop played 5 times, framed the same way. Counter `1/5 … 5/5`.
- (3:42–3:50) The instructor sits relaxed.

**Narration**
> [1:45] Our first movement is the seated knee extension. Choose a sturdy chair without wheels, ideally placed against a wall so it can't slide. Sit tall toward the front of the seat, with both feet flat on the floor and your hands resting on the seat or your thighs.
>
> [2:15] Slowly straighten your right knee, lifting your foot until your leg is as straight as feels comfortable. Pause briefly, then lower it back down with control.
>
> [2:30] One. [2:36] Two. [2:42] Three. [2:48] Four. [2:54] Five.
>
> [3:00] Rest your foot on the floor. Now let's switch to the left leg. Same slow lift, brief pause, and controlled return.
>
> [3:12] One. [3:18] Two. [3:24] Three. [3:30] Four. [3:36] Five.
>
> [3:42] Nicely done. Take a breath before we stand up.

**Safety text (small):** `Straighten only as far as feels comfortable. Stop if you feel sharp pain.`

## Scene 4: Supported Standing Hip Extension (3:50–5:50)

**Lower-third title:** `Supported Standing Hip Extension · 1 set × 5 per side · DRAFT`

**Visuals**
- S4-A (3:50–4:08): the instructor walks to a solid kitchen counter and places both hands on it. Shot as a wide side-on three-quarter view so the feet, hips, and hands are all visible.
- S4-B (4:08–4:22): static setup showing an upright trunk, feet hip-width apart, and soft knees. Overlay: `both hands on counter`, `stand tall`.
- S4-C (4:22–4:52): **right leg**, one-rep loop played 5 times. The leg moves back a short distance (about 15–20° at the hip) while the trunk stays upright with no lower-back arch. Counter `1/5 … 5/5`.
- S4-D (4:52–5:06): reset with the feet together.
- S4-E (5:06–5:36): **left leg**, one-rep loop played 5 times. Counter `1/5 … 5/5`.
- (5:36–5:50) The instructor stands relaxed at the counter.

**Narration**
> [3:50] Next is the supported standing hip extension. Stand facing a sturdy counter, with both hands resting on it for support. Stand tall, feet about hip-width apart, knees soft.
>
> [4:08] Keeping your upper body upright, gently move your right leg backward, just a short distance. You don't need to go far. Then slowly bring it back.
>
> [4:22] One. [4:28] Two. [4:34] Three. [4:40] Four. [4:46] Five.
>
> [4:52] Bring that foot back beside the other one. Now the left leg. Keep your chest lifted and avoid leaning forward.
>
> [5:06] One. [5:12] Two. [5:18] Three. [5:24] Four. [5:30] Five.
>
> [5:36] Well done. If you'd like, take a short rest before the last movement.

**Safety text (small):** `Keep both hands on a stable surface. Stop if you feel unsteady or dizzy.`

## Scene 5: Supported Sit-to-Stand (5:50–7:30)

**Lower-third title:** `Supported Sit-to-Stand · 1 set × 5 (when appropriate) · DRAFT`

**Visuals**
- S5-A (5:50–6:22): wide three-quarter view of a sturdy armchair (or armless chair) against the wall. The instructor sits at the front of the seat with feet hip-width apart, slightly behind the knees, and hands on the armrests. Overlay: `chair against wall`, `feet slightly back`, `hands on armrests (optional)`.
- S5-B (6:22–6:38): a slow demonstration of a single rep with on-screen cue words: `lean forward` → `press through feet` → `stand tall` → `reach back` → `lower slowly`.
- S5-C (6:38–7:18): one-rep loop played 5 times (5 × 8 s) on the same locked angle. Counter `1/5 … 5/5`.
- S5-D (7:18–7:30): the instructor sits relaxed and gives a reassuring smile.

**Narration**
> [5:50] Our final movement is the supported sit-to-stand. Use the same sturdy chair, placed against a wall. Move toward the front of the seat, feet flat and about hip-width apart, slightly behind your knees. You're welcome to use your hands on the seat or armrests for support.
>
> [6:22] Lean your chest slightly forward, press through your feet, and stand up tall. Then reach back for the chair and lower yourself slowly to the seat.
>
> [6:38] One. [6:46] Two. [6:54] Three. [7:02] Four. [7:10] Five.
>
> [7:18] If five feels like too many today, it's fine to do fewer. Stop if anything feels sharp or unsteady.

**Safety text (small):** `Use a chair that cannot move. Fewer repetitions are always fine.`

## Language guardrails (checked against this draft)

- No promises of pain relief, cure, or results. ✔
- No exercise is described as safe for everyone. Fewer reps and skipping are offered as options. ✔
- No diagnosis of knee pain causes, and no claim that weakness is the main cause. ✔
- Medical clearance prompt included (Scene 1). ✔
- Every movement stays within a comfortable range, with stop criteria stated. ✔

## Voiceover direction

Use a warm, unhurried voice at about 125–135 words per minute, leaving natural pauses between instructions. Count reps on the beat of each rep start. The counts must line up with the visible start of each repetition in the edit. Record the voiceover first (human or TTS), lock the timings, then cut the loop clips to the counts.
